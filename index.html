英文作文・20 分・至少 120 字

依圖片比較個人報告與團體報告的優缺點，第二段說明哪種形式對你最有幫助並舉個人經驗。

參考作文 A：團體報告

Students are given many opportunities to make presentations in school, and the ways they do so can be quite different. In Picture A, a student prepares and delivers a presentation individually. She studies the topic, organizes the materials, and speaks in front of the class on her own. This type of presentation helps students become more independent and responsible because they must complete every stage of the task by themselves. It also strengthens research and public-speaking skills. However, it can be stressful since all the pressure falls on one person. In contrast, Picture B shows a group presentation. Several students work together, discuss ideas, and share the responsibility of preparing and presenting. This format allows students to learn teamwork and communication skills while benefiting from one another’s strengths. On the other hand, group members may sometimes disagree on ideas or contribute unequally, making cooperation a challenge.

Among these two forms of presentation, group presentations have been the most helpful to me. Last year, my classmates and I were assigned to give a presentation on renewable energy. At first, I thought the task would be easy, but I soon realized that working with others required patience and effective communication. We divided the work according to our strengths, held several discussions after school, and helped one another improve our parts. During the process, I learned how to express my opinions clearly, listen to different viewpoints, and solve problems through cooperation. Although individual presentations can improve confidence and independence, group presentations have taught me skills that are difficult to learn alone. Since teamwork is essential in both university and future careers, I believe that group presentations have contributed the most to my personal growth and learning.

參考作文 B：個人報告

In school, the ability to present one’s ideas clearly and confidently is regarded as one of the most essential skills a student can develop. The two pictures illustrate contrasting forms of student presentations commonly seen in school settings. In Picture A, a single student stands before a projected screen, delivering a report independently to her seated classmates. Picture B, however, depicts a more collaborative scenario: students work together at a table during the preparation stage, and later a group of them jointly presents to an audience. These two formats each carry distinct advantages and challenges. A solo presentation demands that the speaker master all aspects of the content alone, which cultivates strong self-discipline, independent research ability, and the confidence to hold an audience’s attention without support. The drawback, however, is that the entire burden falls on one person, making anxiety difficult to manage. Group presentations, by contrast, allow members to divide responsibilities and rely on one another, reducing individual stress and encouraging teamwork. The challenge, though, lies in coordinating schedules, reconciling conflicting ideas, and ensuring equal contribution from every member.

Based on my own experience, I find solo presentations more helpful for my personal growth. In my junior year, I was assigned an individual report on climate change for my geography class. Because there was no one else to fall back on, I was forced to research extensively, organize my arguments carefully, and rehearse my delivery repeatedly until I felt truly prepared. The process was demanding, yet it taught me something no group project ever had: that I was capable of standing alone and speaking with conviction. Each time I successfully completed a solo presentation, my self-confidence grew noticeably. For me, it is precisely this kind of independent challenge that transforms a nervous student into a composed and articulate speaker.
